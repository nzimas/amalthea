TRIMUI Brick · Musical instruments

Turn your TrimUI Brick into multiple instruments

Amalthea is not an app on top of the stock firmware. It replaces the Brick's operating system with Amalthea Linux, a minimal distribution built for one purpose: a platform for musical instruments, running them with the lowest latency and the steadiest audio the hardware allows. The gaming frontend, the emulators and the rest of the stock system are gone, and nothing else competes for the processor.

Amalthea nursed Zeus; in the tradition where she is the goat herself, one of her horns became the cornucopia, the horn of plenty. Amalthea is also Jupiter's red moon. The platform offers plenty: synthesis engines, more than seventy effects, generators and a master chain, shared by every instrument and used by each in its own way. New instruments arrive with new builds.

A platform for instruments

Amalthea is built to host a growing fleet of instruments. Underneath all of them sit the same resources: the synthesis engines, the effects, the generators, the master chain, the session banks and the audio and USB stack. Each instrument puts them to a different use: one sequences the engines, another runs effects over external audio, another grows networks of effects. New instruments arrive with new builds, and the installer adds them in place.

On the platform today

Choose an instrument on the home screen. Every one starts blank and stopped; nothing is saved or recalled behind your back.

Tracker

Twelve tracks, each with five independently clocked columns (NOTE, MOD, INST, FX1, FX2), 19 playback directions, conditional events, per-step effects and transposition. Hold X and any column is drawn for you, at random or euclidean, at the density you choose, in 38 scales.

FX

An effect rack for the M-VAVE FM-1's multitrack USB audio. Each of its tracks runs through two inserts, and each insert has a 16-step sequencer of parameter locks, clocked by the FM-1.

Lifeblood

The FM-1's tracks through a generated network of effects, drawn as a living vascular field. Grow it from a recipe, rewire it, and let it mutate on its own.

Why a whole operating system

The stock firmware is a gaming system: a frontend, a service manager, networking, Bluetooth, an overlay filesystem and dozens of background processes. An audio engine must deliver a block every 5.8 milliseconds, and every one of those processes competes with it. We were after the greatest optimisation possible, and that meant owning the whole userspace. Amalthea Linux keeps only what the hardware needs from the stock firmware, and builds everything else itself.

Shared resources

Twelve engines
VAP, PERC, CHIP, FIBER, HIVE, OP4, ANIMA, RUSSOLO, BAY AREA, RESURGENT, CALIGULA and FM DRUM. In the Tracker, each track has its own twelve sounds.
Generators
Melodic idioms, drum patterns, euclidean rhythms, sounds, effects and their parameters, drawn with X at the density you set; fresh entropy on every start. FUNC + hold X gives every step its own variation of its instrument's sound, with the pitch untouched.
Inserts
More than seventy, shared by every instrument: drives and destruction, filters, delays, reverbs, granular, resonators, cuts and repeats, spectral effects, vocoders. Values in real units.
Master
ABRASION, CORROSION, MODULATION, compressor, delay and reverb, with sends from every track.
Banks
Every instrument has its own bank of 64 sessions (the Tracker with 32 patterns per session, LIFEBLOOD with 32 snapshots). Saved only when you save.
FM-1 host
The Brick powers and hosts an M-VAVE FM-1 over USB-C: Bigorna, Tonitor and Lilliput's six tracks, Norilsk's three, and the FM-1's MIDI clock.
USB audio out
Plugged into a computer or phone, the Brick is a class-compliant stereo audio input.
Recovery
A USB recovery shell in RAM, and a one-command return to the stock system.
